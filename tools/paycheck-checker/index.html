Compare gross pay, not take-home

Use the gross pay line on your stub: the amount before taxes and deductions. Take-home pay depends on your tax withholding and benefits, so it cannot be checked from hours alone.

The most common reasons a paycheck is short

  • Hours missing: a shift, or the time before clock-in and after clock-out, never reached payroll.
  • Overtime paid at the regular rate, or two weeks averaged together so that no week shows more than 40 hours.
  • Time clock rounding that always rounds in the employer's favor.
  • A break deducted automatically on days you worked through it.
  • The wrong rate after a raise, or a missing shift differential.

What to do if money is missing

Most gaps are mistakes, and payroll will fix them when you show your own record. Bring the dates, your clock-in and clock-out times and the amount. If it is not corrected, the US Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division and your state labor office take complaints at no cost. This page gives estimates, not legal advice.

Questions

How do I know if my paycheck is correct?

Multiply your regular hours by your rate, add overtime hours at 1.5 times the rate, and compare the result with the gross pay on your paystub. They should match to the cent, or within a few cents when hours are rounded.

Can my employer average my hours over two weeks?

Not for overtime. Under federal law each workweek stands alone. 50 hours in one week is 10 overtime hours even if you worked 30 the week before.

How far back can I claim unpaid wages?

Under federal law it is generally two years, or three years if the violation was willful. Some states allow longer. That is one reason to keep your own record of hours.
